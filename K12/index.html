How to Start

  1. Pick a goal. One outcome. Not three.
  2. Choose 1–2 agents. Start where the friction is sharpest.
  3. Pilot in 30–60 days. One school, real users, narrow scope.
  4. Measure impact. Teacher hours back, attendance, MTSS time, family reach — pick one.
  5. Scale. Add the next agent in the build order. Reuse the backbone.

How to read this table

This table reframes the 118 K–12 AI agents as a periodic system — not a checklist. The position of each agent tells you what it does, how mature it needs to be, and how it relates to the agents around it.

Two axes — domain and autonomy

  • Columns (groups) = the WHAT. Each column is a K–12 function — Enrollment, Attendance, Counseling, Teaching & Learning, Special Populations, Transportation, HR, Budget, Family & Community, Safety, and so on. Read down a column to see how a function progresses from informational chatbots to autonomous, governed systems.
  • Rows (periods) = the autonomy & risk profile. Period 1–2 agents answer questions. Period 3–4 agents complete transactions. Period 5 agents make recommendations. Period 6 agents coordinate across schools and partners. Period 7 agents act with significant autonomy and require the strongest governance.

The two special rows

  • Lanthanides (yellow band, 57–71) — Operational Backbone. Identity, eligibility rules, document OCR, payments rail, master data, SIS/LMS connectors, queue management. These are the shared engines every other agent depends on. They're not user-facing, but they're load-bearing. Underinvest here and everything above gets brittle.
  • Actinides (rose band, 89–103) — Governance, Risk & Public Trust. FERPA Guard, COPPA Compliance, fraud detection, equity impact, AI oversight, Title IX review, IDEA compliance, threat assessment. These cut across every domain. They're not optional — they're the institutional license to operate in K–12.

What K–12 leaders should be considering

  • Build the floor before the ceiling. Period 6–7 agents only work if the lanthanides underneath them are solid. Many districts overinvest in flagship agents (chatbots, tutors) and underfund the shared identity, master-data, and SIS/LMS connectors that make those flagships work at scale.
  • Governance runs in parallel, not sequentially. The actinides aren't a phase 3 deliverable — FERPA, COPPA, equity, and threat assessment need to stand up alongside the first transactional agent that touches student data. In K–12, FERPA and student safety are bright-line risks that don't tolerate "we'll get to it."
  • Sequence by risk, not by enthusiasm. A Period 2 informational agent (Login Help, Family Outreach) carries low risk and ships fast — it builds organizational confidence. A Period 5–7 decision-support or autonomous agent (Risk Radar, Adaptive Curriculum, IEP Orchestrator) needs evidence, governance scaffolding, and human-in-the-loop review before launch.
  • Pick a portfolio, not a project. Use the Start with Your Goal section above to assemble 5–8 agents that together advance one strategic outcome — for example, student wellbeing combines MTSS Intake, Attendance Triage, Risk Radar, Crisis Response, and Population Wellness, supported by Queue Management (backbone) and Equity Impact (governance). One agent in isolation rarely moves the needle.
  • Align to the K–12 pressure stack. Chronic absenteeism, the post-pandemic learning recovery, mental health demand, special ed compliance scrutiny, ESSER cliff, the educator pipeline shortage, AI use policies, and rising cyber and FERPA exposure — this table is designed to map your roadmap to those forcing functions, not against them.
  • Talk in symbols, not slideware. The two-letter symbols (Aa, Bk, Cz) make this a shared vocabulary across superintendent, principals, IT, special ed, and the board. It lowers the activation energy for governance conversations — "Bp needs a Dm before it ships" beats a 30-page deck.
